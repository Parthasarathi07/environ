Method

Five steps, no black boxes

  1. Panel. One row per firm-month: published ESG score, total return, sector, size, realised vol. 160 firms / 108 months, 2016-01 to 2024-12.
  2. Standardise. Each month, z-score the score across firms. 1 SD ≈ 11 rating points.
  3. Sort. Equal-weighted quintiles, rebalanced monthly. Equal weight, because cap-weighting smuggles in a size factor.
  4. Neutralise. Subtract each firm's own sector-month mean. This one step is the whole story.
  5. Test. Quintile spread with a t-stat; then a regression with year fixed effects and standard errors clustered by firm (each firm appears ~96 times — unclustered errors would make a null result look like a discovery).
Definitions are pinned in the code, not invented per chart: "return" = monthly total return compounded; "risk" = vol net of market and sector moves; "spread" = Q5−Q1, gross of costs.

Robustness

The answer moves when the controls move

  • Every confidence interval straddles zero except none of them are tight — with 108 months I am reporting inability to detect, not proof of absence.
  • Sharpe by quintile is nearly flat because the numerator moves as little as the denominator does — a return claim hidden in a risk ratio needs both halves shown.

Honesty test

I published the test my own result fails

The demo panel is generated from three named assumptions. Zero them out and re-run:

python -m environ.data.generate_data \
  --level-bp 0 --momentum-bp 0 --level-vol-pct 0

Ran it. The ESG→volatility coefficient goes -241 bp/SD (t=-8.38) → +10 bp/SD (t=+0.35), and the raw quintile volatility gap collapses to 0.05 pts. The result is the assumed channel, not an artefact.

What flips
The verdict sentences rewrite themselves — "no evidence" becomes "no evidence, and here is the null you would expect". The spread's t-stat collapses toward zero, as it must.
Why I care
This is the test almost no ESG study publishes. If a result cannot fail a null like this, it was never a result — it was a caption. Building the switch took an hour; not shipping it would have been the kind of analysis I would reject from a vendor.

Limitations

Read this slide first in any real conversation

  • Synthetic data. This panel is generated, labelled synthetic in the app, the deck and every figure caption. The method is the deliverable; the magnitudes are illustrative.
  • Fixed universe. Same firms throughout, so no entries or delistings. Low-ESG firms disappear more often — which flatters the naive spread I am criticising, so my conclusion is conservative in that direction only.
  • One rater. ESG scores from different providers correlate poorly. The real follow-up is this same pipeline on two raters side by side; the loader already supports a second panel.
  • Gross of costs. Monthly rebalancing on a momentum signal is not free. A live version needs a turnover budget before the "+3.2%/yr" is investable.
  • Correlation, not causation. Firms that get upgraded may differ in ways I have not measured. I would never present the momentum coefficient as the causal return to "improving ESG".
Small-sample discipline: the correct claim is "on 108 months I cannot detect a return premium and I can detect a risk gradient", not "ESG does not pay".

Recommendation

What I would do with this on Monday

  1. Sell it as downside control, not alpha. Price it as a risk product. The measurable effect is 3.5 points of volatility and -6.4 points of worst-case drawdown.
  2. Tilt on improvement, not standing. Buy rating upgrades, re-screen quarterly, cap turnover.
  3. Never show an ESG return number without the sector-neutral one next to it. That single habit kills most of the noise in this literature.
  4. For a corporate CFO: build the ESG case on cost of capital and volatility, not on growth — the operating data shows no growth or margin benefit.
